The problem

AI hardware is loud. The evidence is quieter.

New processor names arrive every quarter. Most explanations are vivid, confident, and hard to check. XPU-Atlas turns that noise into a decision map you can inspect.

◎

The narrative problem

Twenty labels — CPU, GPU, TPU, NPU, DPU, LPU, QPU, photonic, wetware — are often presented as a flat list of rivals. The history is messier: functions get absorbed, integrated, or abandoned.

⌁

The evidence problem

Only 20.3% of the audited claims rest on primary evidence. Definitions are easy; product specs, launch prices, and financial details are where the narrative gets thin.

↗

The value problem

Research output, public attention, and market value diverge. GPU publications grew 5.4% a year while GPU pageviews fell 18.8%; NPU attention rose 2,196% from a tiny base.

What the evidence shows

Six findings that change the conversation.

Each finding is generated from the same committed data. Click any chart card to jump to the underlying claim ledger or generated results.

01 · Measurement

Acronyms inflate search results.

Unquoted OpenAlex searches overcount by a median of 4.8x. Five terms exceed 10x; PPU reaches 579x.

Inflation factor = unquoted ÷ exact-phrase count. Log scale. Source: OpenAlex snapshot.
02 · Evidence

Primary evidence is improving — slowly.

The primary-source share rises from 6.1% before 2015 to 40.0% for 2021–2026. The overall share is still 20.3%.

Yates χ² = 6.83, p = 0.009, Cramér's V = 0.327. Descriptive signal, not a causal claim.
03 · Lifecycle

Absorption beats replacement.

8 of 20 paradigms (40%) are absorbed into other units or integrated into systems-on-chip. The standalone label often outlives the standalone chip.

Analytical labels, not vendor categories. Counts: absorbed 3, integrated 5, commercial 4, research 4, general-purpose 2, embedded 1, emerging 1.
04 · Attention

Attention is polarising.

NPU and neuromorphic pageviews exploded from tiny bases; CPU, MCU, ASIC, FPGA, and GPU all lost attention.

Change in mean monthly Wikimedia pageviews, 2021→2025. Log-scaled bar length; labels show actual change.
05 · Markets

Value accrues to integrators and the foundry.

Equal-weight CAGR: integrators 33.4%, foundry 30.0%, legacy CPU 4.9%, mobile SoC −0.03%.

Monthly adjusted close, Dec 2021–Nov 2026 where available. Arm starts Oct 2023. Cerebras is a 6-month total return, not annualised: −55.7%.
06 · Architecture

The latency–throughput gap is enormous.

One ordinary CPU: 18.0M serial ops/s, 65.6x with vectorisation, 11,918x with BLAS matmul. Eight threads add only 2.29x.

Intel Core i7-14700F, Python 3.12.13, NumPy 2.2.6. CPU-only benchmark; not a TPU/GPU/QPU measurement.

Who it is for

One atlas. Many jobs.

The same evidence base supports a board briefing, an investment memo, a lesson plan, a procurement review, or a product strategy session.

Strategy & analysts

Pressure-test the narrative

Separate definitions from product claims. See which categories are absorbed, integrated, or still independent before you build a market map.

Investors & VC

Read three signals at once

Publication growth, public attention, and market returns are different signals. The atlas shows where they agree and where they diverge.

Founders & product

Find the integration gap

If 40% of categories are absorbed or integrated, the opportunity may be the system, the software layer, or the workflow — not another standalone chip.

Educators & students

Teach evidence, not slogans

Use the claim ledger, figures, and quiz as a ready-made module on source grading, bibliometrics, and reproducible research.

Journalists & creators

Start from receipts

Every claim has an ID, a status, a grade, and a source pointer. Check the work before repeating the number.

Procurement & enterprise

Ask better vendor questions

Distinguish a primary announcement from a trade-press summary. Request the filing, the benchmark, or the standard behind the slide.

What it is not

Not investment advice, not a causal model, not a live product database, and not a claim that one architecture “wins”. It is a descriptive, reproducible evidence base — with negative and unverified findings deliberately retained.

The data

Frozen, hashed, and ready to reuse.

Three independent snapshots are committed with SHA256 hashes. The default pipeline runs offline; a separate refresh command updates the snapshots from the public APIs.

How it works

Four steps. No magic.

The atlas is a pipeline, not a slide deck. Each stage writes an auditable artifact.

Extract

Turn the narrative into 64 stable, testable claims — one statement at a time.

Grade

Attach a status and an evidence grade: E1 primary, E2 secondary, E3 tertiary, E0 unverified or conflicting.

Join

Add exact-phrase publication counts, public attention, market returns, and a local latency–throughput benchmark.

Reproduce

Freeze the inputs, hash them, and regenerate every table and figure with one command.
